# Morgan Stanley (NYSE: MS) — Investment Memo

**By Archit Chawla — October 19, 2026** | Price: $180.57 (Oct 16 close) | **Recommendation: PASS**

## Business snapshot

Morgan Stanley has spent a decade converting itself from a trading house into a wealth-management compounder — and the market has repriced it accordingly. FY2025 revenue was $66.0B (+14.5% YoY) with $16.2B of net income to common, a 25.6% net margin and ~16.5% ROE. Q3 2026 results were due October 14 (analysts expected ~$2.95 EPS); the street looks for ~$13 EPS for FY2026. The stock trades at 14.3x trailing earnings and 2.67x book — a full rerating from its crisis-era multiples.

## Investment thesis

- **The transformation is real.** Wealth and investment management now anchor the earnings base; FY2022–2025 net income grew from $10.5B to $16.2B with lower volatility than a pure trading house.
- **But the rerating is complete.** At $180.57, the market prices ~5.6% perpetual growth on my payout math — a growth-stock price for a bank.
- **The math does not clear.** My DCF base ($136.93) sits 24% below the quote even with 5.4% five-year earnings CAGR and an 88% payout ratio, because a 10.59% cost of equity discounts it all away.
- **Peer multiples agree with the market, not with me — and that is the point.** Median peer forward P/E (13.3x) implies $182 and peer P/B (2.57x) implies $174, both at the quote. MS is fairly priced *relative to banks*; my call is absolute — the sector's earnings are rate-flattered.
- **The bear case is a market drawdown, not a bank failure.** AUM fees and trading both fall when markets fall; −20% net income in 2026 prices at $58.21.

Note the bull case ($204.19) does clear the quote — it requires the wealth-compounding story to run a decade without a drawdown.

## Key risks

- **AUM leverage cuts both ways.** Wealth-management fees are a fixed percentage of client assets; a 20% market drawdown mechanically removes ~20% of that revenue.
- **Rate normalization.** Net interest income of $10.0B in FY2025 benefits from the 5.1% regime; normalization compresses it while my discount rate stays high.
- **The payout depends on benign markets.** My 88% forecast payout assumes buybacks continue; in a stress year, capital conservation comes first and the DCF's cash flows vanish.

## Recommendation: PASS

A successfully transformed franchise at a price that assumes the transformation never meets a bear market — my DCF base is 24% below the quote, and the market's implied ~5.6% perpetual growth is a bet I cannot underwrite at a 10.6% cost of equity.

## Methodology & verification notes

Banks cannot be valued on enterprise FCF/WACC, so I used the standard bank framework: distributable cash flow (common dividends + share repurchases, from payout ratios on forecast net income) discounted at cost of equity, documented on the Assumptions sheet. I caught and resolved the following: (1) beta 1.22 cross-checked across four sources (Yahoo 1.214, Finnhub 1.23, alcapitaladvisory 1.21, MarketBeat 1.22; wallstreetnumbers 1.41 excluded as a method outlier); (2) the cash-flow statement's "Net Income From Continuing Operations" ($17,025M) exceeds the income statement's Net Income ($16,861M) by $164M in FY2025 — a minority-interest classification difference; the model anchors on Net Income Common Stockholders ($16,249M), which is unaffected; (3) "Cash Dividends Paid" includes preferred dividends, so I subtracted the $612M FY2025 preferred dividend to get distributions to common ($11,816M, 72.7% payout); (4) Yahoo's annual pull carries a blank FY2021 column for MS only — the model uses FY2022–2025 for all four banks; (5) the 10Y yield is already verified at 5.09% (Reuters, Oct 19) and cited as 5.1%; (6) Oct 16 closes were pinned from daily price history, not the live Oct 19 quote.
