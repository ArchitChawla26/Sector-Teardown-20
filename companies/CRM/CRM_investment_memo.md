# Salesforce, Inc. (NYSE: CRM) — Investment Memo

**By Archit Chawla — October 19, 2026** | Price: $240.70 (Oct 16 close) | **Recommendation: HOLD**

## Business snapshot

Salesforce is the global leader in CRM software, selling Sales, Service, Marketing and Commerce clouds plus Data Cloud and its Agentforce AI-agent platform on a seat-based subscription model. FY2026 (ended Jan 31, 2026) revenue was $41.5B (+9.6% YoY) with $7.5B of net income and $14.4B of free cash flow — a 34.7% FCF margin. The company pays a $1.76 annualized dividend and has shrunk diluted shares from 997M to 956M in four years through buybacks.

## Investment thesis

- **A mature compounder with elite cash conversion.** 30.2% EBITDA margins, FCF consistently above net income, and a shrinking share count — the SaaS economics here are proven, not promised.
- **AI optionality is real but unpriced either way.** Agentforce and Data Cloud upsell is the bull case; the bear case is that AI agents shrink per-seat counts even as ARPU rises. My base case splits the difference.
- **The balance sheet changed fast.** Net debt is $33.4B after roughly $24B of debt was added in 2026 — my DCF deducts every dollar of it, which is a meaningful drag versus the FY2026 year-end picture.
- **Fair value, not a bargain.** The base case lands at $223.72, 7.1% below $240.70 — inside my hold band. I would rather buy weakness toward the bear-case zone, or if the 10Y normalizes and the WACC comes down.

Base case: 5-year revenue CAGR of ~7.2% ($41.5B → $58.5B), EBITDA margin expanding 30.2% → 35%, WACC 9.7% (5.1% risk-free + 1.25 beta × 4.5% ERP), 3.0% terminal growth.

## Key risks

- **The $41.7B debt load.** M&A-driven leverage taken on at ~5% rates; de-leveraging is now a prior claim on FCF ahead of buybacks.
- **Seat-based pricing under AI pressure.** If agents do the work of seats, unit economics must be renegotiated customer by customer.
- **Single-digit growth, full multiple.** Any stumble in AI monetization re-rates the stock quickly at 22x trailing earnings.

## Recommendation: HOLD

A great business at a fair price — the DCF base sits just under the quote, so there is no margin of safety to buy, but no reason to sell a compounder of this quality either.
