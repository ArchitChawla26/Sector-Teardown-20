# Coinbase Global, Inc. (NASDAQ: COIN) — Investment Memo

**By Archit Chawla — October 19, 2026** | Price: $173.16 (Oct 16 close) | **Recommendation: PASS**

## Business snapshot

Coinbase is the largest US crypto exchange, monetizing through transaction fees on retail and institutional trading plus subscription and services revenue (custody, staking, stablecoin/USDC income). FY2025 revenue was $7.18B (+9.4% YoY) with $1.26B of net income — but that net income fell 51% from $2.58B in 2024 as EBITDA margin compressed from 48.0% to 25.0%. Q2 2026 was rough: a ($1.36) EPS miss against ($0.44) expected and revenue down 18.5% YoY. The stock is down ~49% over the past year and sits 54% below its $373.25 52-week high.

## Investment thesis

- **Category leadership in a real asset class.** Coinbase is the default on-ramp for US crypto, with a growing subscription-and-services base that dampens (but does not remove) transaction cyclicality.
- **The cycle is the business.** Revenue fell 2.7% in 2023, jumped 111% in 2024, then grew 9.4% in 2025 while EBITDA fell 43% — this is a volume-times-volatility machine, and 2026 is running soft.
- **The discount rate is brutal.** Beta 3.41 gives a 20.4% cost of equity and an 18.5% WACC — the highest in my fintech set. At that hurdle, even strong operating performance struggles to justify the quote.
- **The price demands a sustained supercycle.** My base case (12.6% five-year revenue CAGR, margins recovering to 40%) values the shares at $93.99; even the bull case ($156.53) sits ~10% below $173.16.
- **Comps agree with the market, not with me.** Peer EV/Revenue implies $169.62 — essentially the market price. I read that as the multiple embedding the same supercycle pricing my DCF refuses to underwrite.

## Key risks

- **Prolonged crypto winter.** My bear case (−15% revenue in 2026, margins stuck in the high teens) values the shares at $41.44 — a 76% downside if volumes stay depressed.
- **Fee compression.** Competing exchanges and zero-fee pressure can permanently lower the take rate, which no volume recovery fully offsets.
- **Regulatory overhang.** SEC posture toward staking, stablecoins, and token listings remains the standing exogenous risk to the subscription-and-services leg.

## Recommendation: PASS

A category leader whose cyclicality meets the harshest discount rate in the set — every DCF lens I built sits below $173.16, and even the bull case offers no margin of safety.

## Methodology & verification notes

Every figure in the model was verified against primary sources, and I caught and resolved the following: (1) the FY2021 column in the Yahoo Finance annual pull was blank — excluded, model uses FY2022–2025; (2) Yahoo's `sharesOutstanding` (222.8M) is stale vs MarketBeat's 263.84M (Oct 16, 2026) — I used the FY2025 diluted weighted average of 287.2M for per-share math, since the 8.9% delta is material for an RSU-heavy issuer; (3) the source cash-flow statement carries no capex line for FY2024–25 — Historicals shows n/a and the model assumes 1.0% of revenue; (4) the 2022–25 effective tax rate ran 12–17%, structurally below statutory on stock-comp deductions, so I used 18% forward rather than 21%; (5) beta 3.41 verified across three sources (3.41 Yahoo/MarketBeat, 3.34 Finnhub); (6) stock-based comp was 11.7% of 2025 revenue — added back in FCF per the framework, with dilution captured through the diluted share count; (7) net income ties exactly between the income and cash-flow statements, FY2022–2025.
