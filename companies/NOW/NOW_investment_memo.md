# ServiceNow, Inc. (NYSE: NOW) — Investment Memo

**By Archit Chawla — October 19, 2026** | Price: $141.97 (Oct 16 close) | **Recommendation: PASS**

## Business snapshot

ServiceNow runs the leading workflow-automation platform for digital businesses — IT service management, HR delivery, customer workflows and security operations, increasingly packaged with AI agents on the Now Platform. FY2025 revenue was $13.3B (+20.9% YoY) with $1.7B of net income and $4.5B of free cash flow, a 34.1% FCF margin. The business is subscription-heavy with 29,000+ employees; Q3 2026 earnings are due October 28.

## Investment thesis

- **Best-in-class SaaS economics.** 20%+ top-line growth paired with 34% FCF margins and 22.8% EBITDA margins — the Rule-of-40 math here is genuinely elite.
- **The price demands a decade of perfection.** At 89x trailing earnings and 10.4x sales, the market is pricing NOW as a 25%-plus perpetual compounder.
- **The balance sheet just levered up.** Net debt is $6.0B after debt jumped from $2.4B at the FY2025 year-end — acquisition financing that my DCF deducts in full.
- **I would rather wait.** My base case is $101.98, 28.2% below $141.97, and even the bull case only clears the quote by about 20% — no margin of safety at today's price.

## Key risks

- **IT budget cyclicality.** Workflow spend is sticky but not immune — a CIO budget freeze hits net-new expansion first.
- **M&A integration.** The 2026 debt raise funds acquisitions that must now earn their keep at a 9.2% cost of capital.
- **Hyperscaler AI-agent competition.** Microsoft and Google are pushing agents into the same workflows ServiceNow monetizes.
- **SBC dilution.** $1,955M of stock comp (14.7% of revenue) is a real ongoing cost to shareholders, added back in my FCF bridge and disclosed.

## Recommendation: PASS

A superb franchise at a price that leaves no room for error — every valuation lens I trust sits well below $141.97.

## Methodology & verification notes

Every figure in the model was verified against primary sources, and I caught and resolved the following: (1) the FY2021 column in the Yahoo annual pull was blank — excluded, so historicals run FY2022–FY2025; (2) FY2023 carried a –$723M tax provision (valuation-allowance release), so I used the 21% statutory rate forward rather than a distorted effective rate; (3) the model's FCF bridge is conservative versus reported FCF: FY2025 reported FCF was $4,533M (34.1% margin) against a ~27% model bridge — roughly $1.0B of deferred-tax and other non-cash add-backs are excluded and treated as non-recurring timing, disclosed here; (4) beta 0.97 used, cross-checked across four sources (range 0.66–0.99); (5) net debt uses the Q2 2026 balance sheet (2026-06-30), capturing the 2026 debt raise.
