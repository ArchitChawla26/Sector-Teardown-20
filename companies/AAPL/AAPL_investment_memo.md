# Apple Inc. (NASDAQ: AAPL) — Investment Memo

**By Archit Chawla — October 19, 2026** | Price: $343.46 (Oct 16 close) | **Recommendation: PASS**

## Business snapshot

Apple is the world's most valuable consumer franchise: FY2025 (ended September 2025) revenue was $416.2B (+6.4% YoY) with $112.0B of net income, a 26.9% net margin. iPhone was $209.6B (50.4% of sales); Services hit $109.2B (26.2%) at a 75.4% gross margin against 36.8% for products — the 2.2B+ active installed base converts one-off hardware sales into recurring high-margin profit. Mac ($33.7B) and iPad ($28.0B) both grew; Wearables ($35.7B) was the only category to decline. Q4 FY2026 earnings are due November 2.

## Investment thesis

- **The installed base is the moat.** Services is the only double-digit grower (+13.5% in FY2025) at software margins, and it rides on hardware Apple already sold. This is the best business model in consumer tech.
- **But the cash isn't growing.** Free cash flow was $111.4B in FY2022 and $98.8B in FY2025 — flat to down over four years — while the stock roughly doubled. The multiple expanded, not the cash.
- **The price implies ~7.3% perpetual FCF growth** (my arithmetic, holding my base-case cash path). For a company that grew revenue 6.4% last year on a flat cash base, that is a heroic forever-assumption at a 9.8% discount rate.
- **China is the soft spot.** Greater China revenue fell 4% to $64.4B in FY2025 after an 8% drop in FY2024, and App Store take rates face a US court order plus EU DMA enforcement — the two highest-margin revenue lines are both under pressure.
- **Even my bull case ($217.80) sits 37% below the quote.** An AI-driven iPhone supercycle with margin expansion still can't bridge the gap — there is no plausible DCF path to $343.46 at a 5.1% risk-free rate.

## Key risks

- **The iPhone cycle disappoints.** AI features underwhelm, the upgrade cycle extends, and my 10% FY2026E proves optimistic.
- **China deterioration accelerates** amid local competition and geopolitics — it is ~15% of sales.
- **App Store economics get regulated away** in the US and EU, hitting the highest-margin revenue directly.
- **Multiple compression, not fundamentals.** At 39x trailing earnings, the risk is the P/E normalizing even if the business performs.

## Recommendation: PASS

A phenomenal franchise at a price that demands perpetual 7%+ cash growth on a flat cash base. Every lens I built — DCF in all three cases, peer multiples — sits 37–76% below the quote.
