# SoFi Technologies, Inc. (NASDAQ: SOFI) — Investment Memo

**By Archit Chawla — October 19, 2026** | Price: $14.55 (Oct 16 close) | **Recommendation: PASS**

## Business snapshot

SoFi is a digital-first bank and personal-finance platform: lending (personal, student, home), deposits and checking/savings, investing, and credit cards, all inside one app. FY2025 revenue was $3.61B (+38.3% YoY) with $481M of net income — the second full year of profitability after losses through 2023. Net interest income ($2.22B) is 61% of revenue, so this is a bank wearing a fintech multiple. Q2 2026 beat ($0.12 vs $0.11 expected, revenue +42.5% YoY) and FY2026 EPS guidance is $0.60 — yet the stock sits near its $13.86 52-week low, down ~45% over the past year.

## Investment thesis

- **Genuinely fast growth.** 2022–2025 revenue CAGR of ~32% with net interest income nearly quadrupling — the deposit franchise is working.
- **Profitability just arrived.** Two profitable years do not make a through-cycle record; 2025 net income actually dipped 3.5% YoY despite 38% revenue growth, a reminder that credit costs and reinvestment eat the upside.
- **Banks have to fund their own growth.** Loan-book growth runs through the cash-flow statement, so I value SoFi on an equity-cash proxy: NOPAT plus D&A and stock comp, less capex and a balance-sheet reinvestment charge (20% of revenue growth, judgment). That reinvestment is the wedge between 20% revenue CAGR and shareholder cash.
- **The discount rate does the rest.** Beta 2.33 gives a 15.6% cost of equity and a 13.9% WACC. At that hurdle, my base case (20.1% five-year revenue CAGR, operating margin 23% → 33%) values the shares at $11.62 — 20% below the quote.
- **Comps flatter it.** Peer forward P/E implies $23.64 and EV/Revenue implies $18.00, both above market — but those multiples are set by mature earners, and $0.82 of forward EPS is early-cycle earnings. The DCF, which actually funds the balance-sheet growth, is the binding lens.

The sensitivity sheet shows value is dominated by the WACC and the reinvestment assumption, not by my growth picks.

## Key risks

- **Credit normalization.** SoFi's book has never been tested by a real consumer-credit downturn; my bear case (margins stuck at 18–24%) values the shares at $5.06.
- **Deposit competition.** Funding costs can reprice faster than loan yields if rate competition for deposits intensifies.
- **Dilution treadmill.** Diluted shares grew from 901M (2022) to 1,252M (2025); continued equity-funded growth would dilute the per-share compounding I model.

## Recommendation: PASS

A fast grower at a price my framework cannot reach — the base case sits 20% below $14.55, and even the bull case clears the quote by less than 20%, leaving no margin of safety.

## Methodology & verification notes

Every figure in the model was verified against primary sources, and I caught and resolved the following: (1) the FY2021 column in the Yahoo Finance annual pull was blank — excluded, model uses FY2022–2025; (2) no EBITDA or gross-profit rows exist for a bank, so Historicals and the DCF use an operating-profit proxy of pretax income + D&A (2025: $760.1M, 21.0% margin), labeled as such on every sheet; (3) cash-flow-statement OCF/FCF are deeply negative because loan originations run through operating cash flow — shown as n/a in Historicals and not used; FCF is built as NOPAT + D&A + stock comp − capex − balance-sheet reinvestment (20% of revenue growth, judgment: banks fund loan growth with deposits, so only the equity slice is a shareholder cash cost); (4) 2024 carried a –$265M valuation-allowance tax benefit, so I used the 21% statutory rate forward; (5) beta 2.33 verified across three sources (2.33 Yahoo/MarketBeat, 2.29 Finnhub); (6) net income ties exactly between the income and cash-flow statements, FY2022–2025. All judgment inputs are tagged [JUDGMENT] with a one-line rationale on the Assumptions sheet.
