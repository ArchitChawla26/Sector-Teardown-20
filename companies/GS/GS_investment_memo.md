# The Goldman Sachs Group, Inc. (NYSE: GS) — Investment Memo

**By Archit Chawla — October 19, 2026** | Price: $881.26 (Oct 16 close) | **Recommendation: PASS**

## Business snapshot

Goldman Sachs is the premier global investment bank, monetizing advisory, underwriting, trading, and asset management. FY2025 revenue was $58.3B (+8.9% YoY) with $16.3B of net income to common — a sharp recovery from the $7.9B trough of 2023, which is exactly how cyclical this business is. Q3 2026 results were due October 13 (analysts expected ~$13.87 EPS); the street looks for ~$68 EPS for FY2026 and ~$73 for FY2027. The firm returns essentially all of its earnings: FY2025 total payout was 102.8% of net income.

## Investment thesis

- **Best franchise in capital markets.** When the deal cycle turns, nobody monetizes it like Goldman — FY2024–2025 shows the operating leverage (net income +21% YoY on +9% revenue).
- **But 2025 is a cycle high, and the price assumes it is a plateau.** My base case grows net income at a 5.4% CAGR from the $16.3B peak; the market at $881.26 needs more.
- **The payout is the valuation.** At ~96% forecast total payout, Goldman is a pass-through vehicle; its value is (distributable cash) / (cost of equity − growth), and a 10.91% cost of equity is unforgiving.
- **Relative cheapness is not absolute value.** Peer forward P/E (13.3x) implies $977 and peer P/B (2.66x) implies $991 — both above the quote — but the whole sector is priced on rate-flattered earnings, so winning the relative game still loses money.
- **The bear case is the business model.** Net income fell 27% in 2023; my bear case models a −25% 2026 on a deal freeze, worth $280.37.

Base case: 5-year net income CAGR of ~5.4% from the 2025 cycle high, ~96% total payout ratio (dividends + buybacks), cost of equity 10.91% (5.1% risk-free + 1.29 beta × 4.5% ERP), 3.0% terminal growth. Even with near-total payout, a 10.9% discount rate on cyclical earnings cannot reach $881.26 without heroic perpetual growth.

## Key risks

- **Earnings cyclicality is the thesis risk.** Advisory and underwriting revenue can fall 30%+ in a deal freeze; my bear case is a normal cycle, not a stress test.
- **Rate normalization hits both sides.** Lower rates compress net interest income while the cost of equity stays punitive — a double hit on a bank DCF.
- **Compensation leverage.** In a revenue downturn, the comp ratio rises and the payout ratio I depend on gets cut first.

## Recommendation: PASS

A great franchise priced as if the cycle never turns — my DCF base sits 12% below the quote, and the bull case ($1,196.80) requires a capital-markets supercycle I cannot underwrite. I would rather own it after a deal freeze reprices the earnings base.

## Methodology & verification notes

Banks cannot be valued on enterprise FCF/WACC, so I used the standard bank framework: distributable cash flow (common dividends + share repurchases, from payout ratios on forecast net income) discounted at cost of equity, documented on the Assumptions sheet. I caught and resolved the following: (1) beta 1.29 cross-checked across four sources (Yahoo 1.284, alcapitaladvisory 1.28, MarketBeat 1.29, Defense World 1.29); (2) "Cash Dividends Paid" includes preferred dividends, so I subtracted the $876M FY2025 preferred dividend to get distributions to common ($16,761M, 102.8% payout); (3) the FY2023 earnings trough ($7.9B vs $16.3B in 2025) is carried into the bear case rather than smoothed away; (4) the 10Y yield is already verified at 5.09% (Reuters, Oct 19) and cited as 5.1%; (5) Oct 16 closes were pinned from daily price history, not the live Oct 19 quote.
