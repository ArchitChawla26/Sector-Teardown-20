# JPMorgan Chase & Co. (NYSE: JPM) — Investment Memo

**By Archit Chawla — October 19, 2026** | Price: $339.72 (Oct 16 close) | **Recommendation: HOLD**

## Business snapshot

JPMorgan is the largest bank in the United States by assets and the industry's scale leader across consumer banking, credit cards, and wholesale markets. FY2025 revenue was $181.8B (+7.3% YoY) with $55.7B of net income to common, a 30.6% net margin and ~16.7% ROE — the best through-cycle economics of any money-center bank. Q3 2026 earnings (Oct 13) came in soft: $5.75 EPS vs $5.94 consensus on $50.2B of revenue vs $51.2B expected, and management just raised the quarterly dividend to $1.65 ($6.60 annualized, ~1.9% yield).

## Investment thesis

- **The fortress compounder.** Unmatched deposit franchise and credit underwriting; FY2022–2025 shows net income compounding from $35.9B to $55.7B while buying back ~238M shares.
- **Capital return is the product.** FY2025 total payout was 90.0% of net income ($16.6B dividends + $34.6B buybacks, net of preferred); my forecast holds ~93% — this is where the value lives.
- **A rate beneficiary at peak rates.** Net interest income of $95.4B in FY2025 is flattered by the 10Y at 5.1%; my bear case models the normalization the market is ignoring.
- **Priced about right.** My DCF base ($313.42) trails the quote by 7.7% — inside the HOLD band — and both peer multiples (forward P/E-implied $337.50, P/B-implied $352.34) bracket the $339.72 price.
- **The bull case needs no heroics, just no normalization.** $500.78 on sustained deal activity and buybacks at 100% payout; the bear ($156.06) is what a real NII unwind looks like.

## Key risks

- **Rate normalization compresses the best line.** Every 100 bps of NII compression flows almost straight to distributable cash flow; my bear case (−12% net income in 2026) is the honest version of this.
- **Credit costs lag.** Card and wholesale loss provisions are benign today; a downturn hits both earnings and the buyback authorization.
- **Regulatory capital.** Higher CET1 requirements would mechanically cap the payout ratio my valuation depends on.

## Recommendation: HOLD

The best bank in the world at roughly a fair price — my base case is 7.7% below the quote, inside the HOLD band, and peer multiples confirm the market is not mispricing it in either direction.

## Methodology & verification notes

Banks cannot be valued on enterprise FCF/WACC, so I used the standard bank framework: distributable cash flow (common dividends + share repurchases, from payout ratios on forecast net income) discounted at cost of equity, documented on the Assumptions sheet. I caught and resolved the following: (1) beta 1.02 cross-checked across four sources (Yahoo 1.015, Finnhub 1.04, alcapitaladvisory 1.01, MarketBeat 1.02; abg-analytics 0.90 excluded as stale); (2) "Cash Dividends Paid" includes preferred dividends, so I subtracted the $1,099M FY2025 preferred dividend to get distributions to common ($50,117M, 90.0% payout); (3) the FY2025 diluted average share count (2,781.5M) exceeds the October share count (2,655.6M) — I used the current figure, delta <5%; (4) the 10Y yield is already verified at 5.09% (Reuters, Oct 19) and cited as 5.1%; (5) Oct 16 closes were pinned from daily price history, not the live Oct 19 quote.
