Methodology & verification

I value every company with the same framework so the ranking means something. Each name gets a 5-year explicit DCF (FY2026–2030, with 3-year granular detail), a bull and a bear case, and a comps cross-check against its sector peers. WACC comes from CAPM: 5.1% US 10-year risk-free, 4.5% equity risk premium, per-company beta cross-checked across multiple sources. Terminal growth is 3.0% in the base case. Banks are the one adaptation — you can't run an enterprise DCF on a bank, so I discount distributable cash flow (dividends + buybacks) at the cost of equity instead. That's the standard approach, and it's documented in each model.

The "AI-augmented" part is the workflow: I use LLM tooling to accelerate data extraction and drafting, then verify every figure against primary sources myself. The verification notes in each memo log what I caught — bad share counts, blank history columns, distorted tax rates. If a number couldn't be verified, it's marked N/A with the workaround stated. Nothing is invented.

Recommendations follow the math: BUY needs 20%+ base-case upside with a margin of safety, HOLD is −10% to +20%, PASS is anything worse. The sector report ranks sectors by median implied upside — the conclusion is whatever the numbers say.
